Design things in 3D, without learning CAD

Tavnit 3D is a 3D design app for your printer, your home and your hobbies. Add a box, a bowl or a mug in one click, set dimensions in millimetres, give it a colour, a material and a sticker, and export an STL file to print or render a picture that looks like a photo. Under the hood it's a full parametric CAD, based on Dune 3D, so it keeps up when your projects get more ambitious.

Download for Windows

Windows 10 and 11, 64-bit

On the first install, Windows may show a warning. That's expected - here's how to continue.

From an idea to a printed part, in a few clicks

As easy as Tinkercad to start with, as precise as CAD when you need it.

Ready-made shapes

Box, cylinder, sphere, cone, tube, torus, wedge, hexagon, triangle, star, heart, dome, screw, bowl, vase and 3D text. One click, and the shape stands on the work plane.

Click an object to change it

Clicking a shape selects it and opens a panel with its dimensions, position, colour and material. Drag it to move it, and drag the handles on it to change its height, width and depth.

Exact dimensions

Every dimension is a number you can change at any time, and the object follows. A grid with snapping, Ctrl+D to duplicate and a row of evenly spaced copies help line parts up exactly.

Holes, join, subtract

Like in Tinkercad: turn any shape into a hole, or add a round one, move it into an object and press Cut. You can also merge two shapes into one, subtract one from another, or keep only the part they share.

Hollow and rounded

Hollow out a box, a cylinder or a bowl with walls as thick as you like, open at the top or the bottom, and round every edge, the rim too.

Materials, stickers and patterns

Satin, matte, glossy, metal and see-through glass. A picture of your own, like a logo or a label, stuck onto the surface, round sides included, or as a flat object of its own. And stripes, dots, checks or a grid in the colour you choose.

Lights and photo-like pictures

A lamp, a spotlight and the sun that light the view, and a Render button that makes a striking picture with soft shadows, using the free Blender.

Shapes from a description

Write "a mug with a handle" or "a blue bowl", and an AI builds the shapes, editable afterwards like any other object. Use Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or OpenRouter with your own key, or no key at all: copy a ready prompt into any chatbot and paste its answer back.

Import and export with colours

STL for your printer in one click, STEP for CAD apps, and glTF with colours, materials and stickers for Blender, PowerPoint and the web. Import STEP, IGES, glTF and OBJ with their colours.

Find any command

Ctrl+K, or just start typing, and get any command with where it is in the window and its keyboard shortcut.

Updates itself

Every time it starts, the app checks for a new version. Clicking "Update now" downloads it, installs it and restarts.

Your work is backed up

Every few minutes, unsaved changes are copied aside. If the computer crashes or the power goes out, the app offers your work back the next time it starts.

Not just for beginners

Starting is easy, but you don't have to stop there. Under the ready-made shapes is the full parametric CAD of Dune 3D, for parts with real requirements: brackets, enclosures, spare parts and mechanisms.

Sketches with constraints

Draw lines, arcs, circles and curves, on the work plane or right on an object's face, and pin them down with dimensions, angles, and parallel, perpendicular, tangent, equal and symmetric constraints. Pull them out, or push in to cut.

More than extrude

Revolve a profile, loft between shapes, and sweep or pipe a profile along a path.

Arrays and mirrors

Repeat a feature in a row or around a circle, and mirror it, so a part with many holes or teeth stays one simple design.

Fillets and chamfers

Round or bevel exactly the edges you choose, not only all of them at once.

A history that stays editable

Every step is kept. Go back to a sketch or a dimension from the start, change it, and everything built on it follows.

Works with other CAD

Import and export STEP with exact geometry, import IGES, and bring in DXF drawings as sketches.

Where the code comes from, and who wrote it

Based on Dune 3D

Tavnit 3D is a fork of Dune 3D, an open-source CAD app written by Lukas K. and other contributors, licensed under GPL-3.0. The foundations are theirs: the Open CASCADE geometry kernel, the SolveSpace constraint solver and a GTK 4 interface. Tavnit 3D keeps everything Dune 3D can do, adds a simpler way of working on top, and builds a ready-to-install version for Windows.

The changes were written mostly by Claude

Most of the code Tavnit 3D adds was written by Claude, an AI model by Anthropic. The direction and the decisions are Star's: what to build, how it should work, testing in real use, and what goes into a release. Every change goes through a build and automated tests, and the full history is public on GitHub.

Not an official Dune 3D release

Found a problem in Tavnit 3D? Please report it here, not to the original project. General improvements that suit Dune 3D too are offered back to it as pull requests.

Installing without worry

The first time you run the installer, Windows will probably show a blue screen saying it "protected your PC". That doesn't mean anything is wrong. It's how Windows reacts to any new app from an independent developer. Here's how to continue:

  1. 1
    Click "More info" It's a small link under the text.
  2. 2
    Click "Run anyway" The button only appears after the previous step.
  3. 3
    The installation starts It doesn't ask for admin rights: by default the app is installed for your user only, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Tavnit 3D. You can choose to install it for all users on the setup screen.

Why is there a warning at all?

Windows recognises apps by a digital signature - a certificate developers buy for a few hundred dollars a year - and by reputation built up over many downloads. Tavnit 3D is a free personal project and doesn't have such a certificate. So the warning only says "Windows doesn't know this app yet", not "something dangerous was found in it".

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to use it?

A PC with Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. A mouse with a scroll wheel makes turning and zooming the view especially comfortable.

Is it only for beginners?

No. The ready-made shapes and the object panel make the start easy, but the full parametric CAD of Dune 3D is right there: sketches with constraints, revolves, lofts, sweeps, arrays, mirrors, fillets and chamfers on chosen edges, and STEP files. You can start with a box and move on to precise parts when you need them.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. Tavnit 3D is completely free, with no ads. The code is open source under GPL-3.0, like Dune 3D, which it's based on.

What language is the app in?

English, like Dune 3D. The installer comes in English and Hebrew, and this site is also available in Hebrew.

How do I update to a new version?

Every time it starts, the app checks for a new version, and if there is one, a strip appears above the work area. Click "Update now" and the app downloads the version, installs it and opens again. Save your open files first. You can turn the automatic check off in the main menu, and you can always download the latest version here and install it over the existing one.

What do I need for the Render button?

Blender, a free app you install separately from its website. Tavnit 3D finds it by itself and runs it in the background when you click Render. A graphics card makes it faster, but it works without one too.

What do I need to make shapes from a description?

Either an API key for an AI service - Claude (Anthropic), ChatGPT (OpenAI), Gemini (Google), OpenRouter, or any OpenAI-compatible address - or nothing at all. With a key, use is billed by that service, usually a few cents per shape, and the key is kept on your computer only. Without one, "Copy prompt for a chatbot" copies a ready request; paste it into any chatbot, copy its answer and click "Paste the answer". Either way, what you write is sent to the service you chose.

Which files can I import?

STEP and IGES from CAD apps, and glTF/GLB and OBJ from 3D apps like Blender, with their colours and materials. Each colour and material in the file becomes an object of its own that you can change. Objects from glTF and OBJ are triangle meshes, so they can't be joined or subtracted, but you can move, colour and export them.

Can I open Dune 3D files?

Yes, Tavnit 3D uses the same file type (.d3ddoc). The other way round, a file saved in Tavnit 3D may not open in an older version of Dune 3D. The app warns you about this when it matters.

Where are my files saved, and what if the app crashes?

Saving and opening start in the "Tavnit 3D" folder inside Documents, and you can choose another folder in the main menu. Every few minutes, documents with unsaved changes are also copied to an "Autosave" folder there (how often, or not at all, is up to you in the main menu). If the app closes unexpectedly, it offers that work back the next time it starts.

How do I uninstall it?

In Windows Settings, under "Apps", choose Tavnit 3D and click "Uninstall". Files you saved are not deleted.

I found a problem or have an idea

Glad to hear it. You can open a report on the GitHub issues page.
